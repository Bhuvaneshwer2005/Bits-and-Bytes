# Problem statement (draft, app name TBD)

## The user
A first- or second-year college student with lecture-note PDFs and a packed day. They study in gaps: a bus ride, a walk, a queue. Their phone is the only device they have in those moments.

## The pain
- Re-reading notes feels productive but is passive, so little sticks.
- Active recall (flashcards, quizzes) works, but making cards by hand from a 30-page chapter takes an hour they don't have.
- Reading on a phone in a gap is hard. Listening is easy.
- Study habits fade because nothing rewards showing up each day.

## Our solution
Drop in a notes PDF. In about a minute it becomes a spoken, captioned flashcard deck, then a quiz on exactly what you just went through, then a one-page cheat sheet. A daily streak and calendar heatmap reward coming back. No chat window: the interface is the cards, the voice and the heatmap.

## Why it is trustworthy
Every card and question shows its source page. A second AI pass flags anything the PDF doesn't support. Users can edit or regenerate any card, and the app says plainly that a deck is AI-generated.

## What success looks like (for the demo)
- PDF to first playing card in about 60 seconds, or instantly with the sample deck.
- A student can finish deck + quiz in one commute (about 10 minutes).
- Works on a phone, with captions, large text and big tap targets.

## 3-minute demo outline (rubric: presentation)
1. 20 s: the user and the pain.
2. 20 s: drop the PDF (or open the sample deck), show the progress steps.
3. 60 s: play cards with voice and captions, flip one, show its source page and a flagged card.
4. 40 s: quiz, then the results screen with streak and heatmap.
5. 20 s: edit a card and export the cheat sheet.
6. 20 s: how AI is used responsibly (prompts, source pages, hand edits).
